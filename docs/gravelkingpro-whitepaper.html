The Problem

Artists Are Creating. Nobody Is Protecting Them.

The music industry is in a legal crisis. AI tools have made music creation accessible to millions — but the legal infrastructure hasn't kept up. Here's what every independent artist is up against right now:

⚠️

No Proof, No Rights

The U.S. Copyright Office requires demonstrable human authorship to grant copyright. Without documented proof of your contribution, your work is unprotected — anyone can sample, copy, or sell it.

🤖

AI Gets the Credit

Platforms like Suno, Udio, and others generate music at scale. When AI makes the track and a human "publishes" it, there's no mechanism to distinguish creative ownership — everything looks the same to the law.

💸

Stolen Revenue

Without enforceable IP, artists can't pursue DMCA takedowns, licensing disputes, or sync licensing deals. Estimated royalty losses from unprotected AI-assisted music exceeded $180M in 2023.

🚪

Locked Out of Professional Tools

Professional studios charge $150–$500/hour. Independent artists get inferior tools. No single platform combines pro-grade production with rights protection at an accessible price point.

The Ruling That Changes Everything

In 2023, the U.S. Copyright Office issued a formal policy statement: "Human authorship applied to AI-generated material qualifies for copyright protection." This means artists who can document their creative contribution to AI-assisted music have legal standing — but only if they can prove it. GravelKingPro is built around that proof.

IP Protection Strategy

Protect Your Work. For Free.

You don't need a lawyer or a patent filing fee to protect what you've built right now. These are the most effective moves, in order of priority, all doable this week at zero to minimal cost.

1

Defensive Publication (Do This Today — Free)

Write a detailed public description of how your authorship scoring system works — what it does, the general approach, the threshold system, the certificate output — and publish it on a timestamped public platform (your website, Medium, GitHub README, or Reddit r/MachineLearning). Then archive it at web.archive.org. This creates "prior art" that prevents ANY company — including Apple, Spotify, or Google — from patenting this concept after your publish date. You can't patent it yourself this way, but nobody else can either. This is the single highest-leverage move you can make today.

Cost: $0 · Time: 2 hours
2

Register Your Software Copyright ($65)

Go to copyright.gov and file Form TX (literary works, which covers software). Upload a representative portion of your source code. This gives you legal standing to sue for statutory damages up to $150,000 per infringement — without registration you can still sue, but you're limited to actual damages (which are hard to prove and usually small). $65 is the single best legal investment you can make. It takes about 20 minutes to file online.

Cost: $65 one-time · Time: 20 minutes
3

Lock Your Algorithm as a Trade Secret (Free)

MLK V3's internal scoring logic should never be published openly. Keep it proprietary. Add a strong Terms of Service clause prohibiting reverse engineering, and use environment variables (never exposed in client-side code) for any secret parameters. As long as you take reasonable steps to keep it secret, trade secret law protects it indefinitely — longer than any patent. This is how Coca-Cola has protected its formula for 130+ years.

Cost: $0 · Ongoing practice
4

Timestamp Your Codebase Evidence (Free)

Your GitHub commit history is already a timestamped legal record of when you built each feature. Make your repository private (or keep it private) but preserve all commit history. Email yourself a summary of what the platform does — date, features, how it works — via Gmail (which timestamps emails server-side and is difficult to forge). Forward it to a few trusted people. This creates a paper trail of your creation timeline.

Cost: $0 · Time: 30 minutes
5

File a Provisional Patent When You Can ($320)

A provisional patent application at USPTO.gov does NOT require a lawyer. It locks in your priority date for 12 months, gives you "Patent Pending" status, and costs $320 for small entities. Within those 12 months, you can file the full utility patent yourself (for another ~$800) or find a patent attorney to help. The provisional just needs to describe the invention clearly — your authorship scoring method, the threshold system, and the certificate generation are likely patentable as a novel combination. USPTO.gov has free guides for pro se (self-represented) filers.

Cost: $320 when budget allows · Time: 1–2 weeks to write
