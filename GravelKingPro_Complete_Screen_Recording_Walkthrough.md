# GravelKing Pro: Complete Beginner-to-Label Walkthrough

## A word-for-word screen-recording script

**Purpose:** This is a peer-to-peer script for someone demonstrating GravelKing Pro to a person who has never used it before.

**Starting point:** A blank web browser.

**Ending point:** A finished song is generated, reviewed, mastered, downloaded, and submitted to GravelKing Productions for label review.

**Suggested video length:** 15–25 minutes, depending on generation and mastering time.

---

## Before recording

Have these items ready:

1. A working email address for account creation.
2. A song idea. The example in this script is provided below.
3. A square JPEG or PNG cover image for the label-submission section.
4. Enough account credits to generate and master the song.
5. A Pro or King account if you want to demonstrate label submission.

You do **not** need to know songwriting, recording, mixing, or mastering terminology. This walkthrough explains each step in everyday language.

> **Important:** Only certify music as original work when you own the material and have the right to make that declaration. Do not certify a cover, karaoke recording, or unauthorized remix as your original work.

# PART 1 — OPEN THE WEBSITE

## Scene 1: Start from a blank browser

### ON SCREEN

Open a new browser tab. Click the address bar and type:

**gravelkingpro.com**

Press **Enter**.

### SAY

“I’m starting from the very beginning, as if I’ve never used this website before. I’m opening a browser, typing `gravelkingpro.com`, and pressing Enter.

This is the GravelKing Pro home page. The main idea is right here: ‘Make it. Prove it.’

GravelKing Pro is a workspace for creating a song, documenting the creative process, recording vocals, mastering the finished audio, and preparing it for release. I don’t need to understand professional studio software before I start.”

### ON SCREEN

Slowly move the pointer over the four tool cards:

- **The Foundry**
- **JAX**
- **Vocal Booth**
- **Converter**

### SAY

“The website is organized around four main tools.

The Foundry is the mastering tool. That is where I polish a finished song and create the final audio file.

JAX is the songwriting companion. That is where I can develop an idea, write lyrics, and generate a song.

Vocal Booth lets me load a backing track, record my own vocal, and create a vocal-and-music mix.

Converter changes an audio file from one format to another.

For this walkthrough, I’m going to begin with nothing but an idea, use JAX to turn that idea into a song, master it, download the final file, and then submit it to the GravelKing Productions label.”

The account matters because generated songs, credits, downloads, and label submissions need to be connected to me. Once the sign-in is complete, I return to the GravelKing Pro workspace.”

# PART 4 — TURN A SIMPLE IDEA INTO LYRICS

## Scene 4: Meet JAX

### ON SCREEN

Return to JAX. Point to the heading:

**What are we writing today?**

Click the **Message JAX…** field.

### SAY

“This is JAX, the songwriting companion.

The screen asks, ‘What are we writing today?’ I can describe a story, a mood, a genre, a hook, or even one unfinished line. I do not have to use technical language.”

### ON SCREEN

Type this example prompt:

> Help me write a soulful country-rock song about rebuilding my life after everybody counted me out. I want the chorus to feel victorious, but not arrogant. Use plain language, strong imagery, and a hook people can remember after hearing it once.

Click the upward arrow to send the message.

### SAY

“I’m going to type a normal description of the song I want.

I’m saying: ‘Help me write a soulful country-rock song about rebuilding my life after everybody counted me out. I want the chorus to feel victorious, but not arrogant. Use plain language, strong imagery, and a hook people can remember after hearing it once.’

Now I click the arrow to send it.”

### ON SCREEN

Wait while the message **JAX is writing…** appears.

When the response appears, scroll through it slowly.

### SAY

“JAX is now helping me shape the idea. I can read the response myself, or I can click ‘Read aloud’ and listen to it.

This is a conversation, so I don’t have to accept the first answer. I can ask JAX to rewrite one line, make the chorus simpler, change the point of view, or make the song more personal.”

---

## Scene 5: Refine the song in plain language

### ON SCREEN

Type a follow-up message such as:

> Make the chorus shorter. Keep the idea of rebuilding, but give me one repeated line that could become the title.

Send the message.

### SAY

“I want a shorter chorus, so I’m asking for exactly that.

I’m typing: ‘Make the chorus shorter. Keep the idea of rebuilding, but give me one repeated line that could become the title.’

I can continue this process until the song sounds like something I would actually say.”

### ON SCREEN

When the revised response appears, click **Save to song**.

### SAY

“When I find a version I want to keep, I click ‘Save to song.’ That carries the JAX response into the song area below.

The important thing to understand is that JAX is not asking me to become a software expert. I am giving creative direction in ordinary language, reviewing the result, and making the decisions.”

---

# PART 5 — GENERATE THE AUDIO

## Scene 6: Review the Song Generator

### ON SCREEN

Scroll down to:

**Song Generator — Turn the lyrics into a finished take**

Point to **Lyric input** and **Style prompt**.

### SAY

“Now I’ve reached the Song Generator.

The lyrics I saved from JAX appear in the Lyric input box. I can read through them and make any last changes directly in this box.

The Style prompt describes how I want the recording to sound. This is where I describe the genre, tempo, instruments, vocal character, mood, and production style.”

### ON SCREEN

In the **Style prompt** box, type:

> Soulful country rock, around 88 BPM, warm male lead vocal, acoustic guitar in the verses, full drums and electric guitar in the chorus, emotional but hopeful, clear modern mix, strong final chorus.

### SAY

“My style description says: ‘Soulful country rock, around 88 BPM, warm male lead vocal, acoustic guitar in the verses, full drums and electric guitar in the chorus, emotional but hopeful, clear modern mix, strong final chorus.’

Notice that I’m describing what I want to hear. I’m not copying a specific artist. I’m giving musical direction.”

### ON SCREEN

Point to the generation choices. Select the desired generator:

- **Generate with MLK — 20 credits**, or
- **Generate with JAX — 20 credits**

### SAY

“At the bottom, I choose a generation option. Either finished-song option uses 20 credits.

For this walkthrough, I’m selecting the JAX generation option and clicking the button once.

I do not refresh the page or click repeatedly while it is generating. I leave the page open and wait for the take to finish.”

### ON SCREEN

Wait for the completion message explaining that the take is in the library.

### SAY

“The message now tells me the render is complete and the take has been placed in My Library.

At this point, I started with one paragraph describing an idea, and GravelKing Pro has turned that idea into a playable song.”

# PART 6 — REVIEW THE SONG IN MY LIBRARY

## Scene 7: Open the generated track

### ON SCREEN

Open **My Library** from the navigation.

Select the newly generated track.

### SAY

“I’m opening ‘My Library.’ This is where my generated and purchased tracks are kept.

I select the new track. Now I can see the song title, artist name, cover area, playback controls, lyrics, and download options.”

### ON SCREEN

Click the round **Play** button. Let several seconds play.

Move the position slider briefly, then pause.

### SAY

“I click Play and listen to the result.

This is the review stage. I’m listening for the basic things anybody can hear: Do I like the performance? Can I understand the vocal? Does the chorus feel bigger than the verse? Is the song too long? Is the mood right?

I don’t have to master the first version. If the creative direction is wrong, I can return to JAX, change the lyrics or style prompt, and generate another take.

For this demonstration, I’m happy with this version, so I’m ready to master it.”

---

# PART 7 — MASTER THE TRACK

## Scene 8: Send the song to The Foundry

### ON SCREEN

Click **Master this track**.

Wait for the Mastering page to load the generated track.

### SAY

“I click ‘Master this track.’

That sends the selected song directly into the GravelKing Pro Mastering tool, also called The Foundry.

Mastering is the final polish applied to a finished mix. It controls the overall tone, dynamics, stereo balance, and loudness so the track is ready to deliver.”

---

## Scene 9: Choose simple mastering settings

### ON SCREEN

Point to the preset controls. Choose the preset that best matches the song. If uncertain, use the normal or balanced preset shown in the interface.

Leave advanced controls at their default values for a beginner walkthrough.

### SAY

“The first choice is the mastering preset. I pick the preset that best matches the type of song I made.

If I’m a beginner and I don’t understand the advanced controls, I leave them at their recommended defaults. I do not need to adjust every slider to get a finished result.

There is also an optional Denoise control. That is mainly useful when a recording has audible hiss, hum, or room noise. Because this is a generated track and it is already clean, I’m leaving Denoise off.”

---

## Scene 10: Decide whether to certify the work

### ON SCREEN

Point to:

**Certify this as my original work**

If this is truly original and owned by the demonstrator, turn it on. Otherwise, leave it off.

### SAY

“This next choice is important.

The checkbox says, ‘Certify this as my original work.’ I only turn this on when the song is my original work and I have the right to make that statement.

I leave this off for a cover song, karaoke performance, or remix that uses material I do not fully own.

For this demonstration, I’m treating this as my original project, so I’m turning certification on.”

### ON SCREEN

Choose the appropriate certification category.

If you have legitimate industry identifiers, enter them:

- **IPI Number** for the songwriter
- **ISWC** for the composition
- **ISRC** for the recording

Otherwise, leave those optional fields blank.

### SAY

“I choose what I’m certifying.

The IPI, ISWC, and ISRC boxes are optional. I only enter those identifiers if they have already been legitimately assigned to me or this work. I never invent these numbers.

The certificate creates technical evidence connected to the file, including hashes, timestamps, and verification data. It supports a chain of custody, but it does not replace copyright registration, legal advice, or worldwide catalog clearance.”

---

## Scene 11: Describe your human creative direction

### ON SCREEN

In **Instrumental Style Prompt**, enter a detailed description such as:

> Soulful country-rock arrangement at 88 BPM with acoustic guitar carrying the verses, a restrained kick and snare groove, electric guitar opening the chorus, warm lead vocal, hopeful lift in the final chorus, and a clean modern stereo mix.

### SAY

“The Instrumental Style Prompt records the creative direction I gave the project.

I describe the tempo, instruments, arrangement, vocal character, mood, and how the song should develop.

The more specific and truthful I am, the clearer the record of my creative choices becomes.”

---

## Scene 12: Run the master

### ON SCREEN

Click:

**Master with [selected preset]**

Wait while the track processes.

### SAY

“Everything is ready, so I click ‘Master with’ the preset I selected.

Mastering and downloading the final master uses 75 credits. I click once and leave the page open while the audio processes.

GravelKing Pro now applies the Morris Law Kernel mastering chain to the track.”

### ON SCREEN

When complete, show the before-and-after player.

Play a few seconds of **Before**, then switch to **After**.

### SAY

“The master is complete.

I can compare the original version against the mastered version. I’m listening for a more controlled low end, clearer detail, consistent loudness, and an overall presentation that feels finished without destroying the character of the song.”

---

# PART 8 — DOWNLOAD THE FINISHED PRODUCT

## Scene 13: Save the master

### ON SCREEN

Click the **Download** button for the mastered WAV.

Show the browser’s download confirmation or downloaded file.

### SAY

“Now I click Download.

The mastered WAV is the finished high-quality audio file. This is the file I keep as my main master.

If I also need an MP3 for quick sharing, I can use the MP3 option in My Library or use the Converter. But I keep the WAV because it is the better source file for distribution, archiving, and future conversions.”

### ON SCREEN

If certification was enabled and certificate documents are available, open the certificate panel and download the available PDF.

### SAY

“Because I enabled certification, I also save the certificate document that belongs with this track.

I keep the mastered WAV, the certificate, the lyrics, the cover art, and any industry identifiers together in one folder. That gives me an organized release package instead of a loose audio file with no supporting record.”

# PART 9 — OPTIONAL: RECORD YOUR OWN VOCAL

## Scene 14: Explain the Vocal Booth path

> This section is optional. Skip it if the generated vocal is the final performance.

### ON SCREEN

Open **Vocal Booth** from the navigation.

### SAY

“If I want to replace the generated vocal or sing over my own backing track, I open the Vocal Booth.

The Vocal Booth is optional. I do not need it when I am keeping the finished generated performance.”

### ON SCREEN

Upload the backing track. Allow microphone access when the browser asks.

Paste or load the lyrics.

### SAY

“First I add the backing track.

When the browser asks for microphone permission, I choose Allow. I recommend headphones so the backing track does not bleed into the microphone.

I can add my lyrics and use automatic timing when it is available. If automatic timing is not available, I can use tap-to-time and tap when each line begins.”

### ON SCREEN

Start a take, perform briefly, stop, and preview it.

### SAY

“I start the take, sing along with the backing track, and stop when I’m finished.

Then I preview the vocal and backing together.

When I’m happy with the performance, I use the mix export option. The full vocal-and-backing mixdown requires the King plan.”

# PART 10 — PREPARE THE LABEL SUBMISSION PACKAGE

## Scene 15: Explain what the label form requires

### SAY

“I now have the finished audio. Before I submit it to the label, I need three files:

First, the full-length mastered track.

Second, a preview clip no longer than 30 seconds.

Third, square cover art in JPEG or PNG format.

I also need the exact track title and artist name I want displayed.”

### ON SCREEN

Show the three files in a folder:

1. Full mastered WAV
2. 30-second preview audio
3. Square cover image

### SAY

“I keep these clearly named so I do not upload the wrong files.

My full track might be named `song-title-master.wav`.

My preview might be named `song-title-preview.mp3`.

My cover might be named `song-title-cover.png`.”

# PART 11 — SUBMIT TO GRAVELKING PRODUCTIONS

## Scene 16: Open the label

### ON SCREEN

Return to GravelKing Pro.

Open **Label** from the navigation or footer.

### SAY

“Now I open the Label section.

This is GravelKing Productions, the independent label side of the platform. The catalog displays approved tracks and allows visitors to preview available music.

Submitting a track requires an eligible paid account.”

### ON SCREEN

Click **Submit Your Track** or **Submit to Label**.

### SAY

“I click ‘Submit Your Track.’

The website checks my subscription and submission eligibility before opening the form.”

---

## Scene 17: Complete every field

### ON SCREEN

Fill in **Track Title**.

Fill in **Artist Name**.

Upload **Full Audio File**.

Upload **Preview Clip**.

Upload **Cover Art**.

### SAY

“I enter the track title exactly as I want the public to see it.

Next, I enter my artist name.

Under Full Audio File, I upload the full-length mastered track. The form accepts common audio formats, and the full file can be up to 150 megabytes.

Under Preview Clip, I upload a short clip that is no longer than 30 seconds. I choose a section that gives the listener a strong idea of the song—usually part of the chorus.

Under Cover Art, I upload a square JPEG or PNG image.

Before I continue, I check the spelling of the title and artist name, confirm that the full file is the master, confirm that the preview is 30 seconds or shorter, and confirm that the cover is the correct image.”

---

## Scene 18: Submit for review

### ON SCREEN

Read the submission notice, then click **Submit Track** once.

### SAY

“The notice explains that Pro subscribers can submit once every seven days. Node Auditor accounts can have unlimited submissions.

I click ‘Submit Track’ one time and wait for the upload to finish.

I do not close the page while the files are uploading.”

### ON SCREEN

Show the success message.

### SAY

“The submission is complete.

In the normal artist workflow, the track is now pending admin approval. It does not automatically become a public label release just because I uploaded it.

The label reviews the submission before it appears in the store. If my account has a submission cooldown, the page tells me when I can submit the next track.”

---

# PART 12 — WHAT HAPPENS AFTER APPROVAL

## Scene 19: Return to the label catalog

### ON SCREEN

Click **Back to Label** or open the Label page.

Scroll to **Label Catalog**.

### SAY

“After the label approves the track, it can appear in the GravelKing Productions catalog.

The public listing can show the track, artist, artwork, and preview. A listener can hear the preview and purchase an available track through the store.

That completes the full path:

I started by typing `gravelkingpro.com`.

I created or signed into my account.

I described a song idea to JAX.

I refined the lyrics and creative direction.

I generated the song for 20 credits.

I reviewed it in My Library.

I mastered and downloaded it for 75 credits.

I saved the supporting certificate when certification applied.

I prepared a full master, a 30-second preview, and square cover art.

And finally, I submitted the finished release package to GravelKing Productions for label review.”

# SHORT VERSION FOR A 60–90 SECOND VIDEO

### SAY

“Let me show you how GravelKing Pro takes you from a song idea to a finished label submission.

Start by going to `gravelkingpro.com` and opening JAX.

Tell JAX what kind of song you want in plain language. Review the response, ask for changes, and save the lyrics you want to keep.

Add a style prompt describing the genre, tempo, instruments, mood, and vocal character. Generate the finished take for 20 credits.

Open My Library, listen to the song, and click ‘Master this track.’

Choose a mastering preset. If the song is completely original and you own it, you can turn on certification and document your creative direction. Do not certify covers or unauthorized remixes.

Run the master and download the finished WAV. Mastering and downloading uses 75 credits.

To submit to GravelKing Productions, prepare the full master, a preview clip no longer than 30 seconds, and square cover art.

Open Label, click ‘Submit Your Track,’ enter the title and artist name, upload all three files, and submit.

The label reviews the track before it goes live. That’s the full process—from typing `gravelkingpro.com` to a finished song submitted for release.”
