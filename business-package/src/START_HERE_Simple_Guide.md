# Start Here — Simple Guide to Your Package

This guide explains, in plain language, what is in your business package and how to use it. Read this first.

## What you have

You have a complete "go to market" kit for one product: **Morris Law Kernel v3.5** — your audio mastering engine that also stamps songs with proof of who made them.

Think of the package as three boxes:

1. **Documents that explain the product** — you send these to people who might pay you (investors, software companies, music platforms).
2. **Documents that help you sell** — ready-made messages, posts, and email scripts so you never start from a blank page.
3. **Documents that keep you organized** — checklists and trackers so nothing falls through the cracks.

## The first three things to do

1. **Watch your own pitch.** Open the pitch deck (now a real slide presentation) and read it out loud twice. This is your story — know it cold.
2. **Start the 7-day posting plan.** The posts are already written. Copy one per day to X, LinkedIn, and Facebook.
3. **Send 10 LinkedIn messages.** The messages are already written too. Log every send in the Outreach Tracker.

## Words you will see (simple meanings)

- **Mastering** — the final polish that makes a song loud, clear, and ready for release.
- **Watermark** — an invisible stamp hidden inside the audio that says "this is mine."
- **Provenance** — the proof trail of who made a file and when.
- **Licensing** — letting a company use your technology for a fee. You keep ownership.
- **Pilot** — a small paid trial with one company before a bigger deal.
- **LOI (Letter of Intent)** — a written "we are seriously interested" note. Not a contract yet, but strong.
- **Data room** — a tidy folder of documents you share when someone is close to paying or investing.
- **NDA** — an agreement that they will not share your secrets.

## One honest note

The revenue numbers in the Financial Projections are **estimates and targets**, not promises. They are a reasonable starting point for conversations. Update them with real numbers as soon as you have real deals — real numbers, even small ones, are always more convincing.
